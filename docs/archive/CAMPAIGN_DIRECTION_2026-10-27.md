## Established boundaries

The Roman baggage rescue is the first tutorial. The centurion and two brothers are Rome-born, use similar equipment, and retain their established relationship and combat identities. Capture ends this mission; a later sale to a disguised alien remains established. Names, ages, specific legion, buyer/captor identities, and reveal staging remain open.

The godlike enforcers and galactic bureaucracy are the same society (D32). The Confederation is the accepted elaboration of that society into an institution of Tier One powers (D36). Catastrophic-war prevention and the earlier universe-ending concern can coexist; exact threats, membership, and government are undefined.

The contact restriction remains established, but D33/D35 reopen illegal abduction, species-dependent permission for slavery, and a proxy-war loophole. Do not use those claims as settled explanations for game rules.

The early alien veteran teaches advanced-technology consequences (D34). He is neither the centurion nor an added member of the Roman rescue detachment. His exact mission, identity, species, augmentation, and fate are undecided. Scripted death and permanent loss are not approved requirements.

## Proposed systems and implementation boundaries

| Topic | Current direction | Before implementation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Power versus time (P10) | Equipment and augmentations may improve capability while increasing signature and extraction pressure. | Choose a separate off-world test and explicitly provisional rules. Keep signature generation, detection, and enforcement response distinct. |
| Experiment alternatives | Compare Roman equipment, one advanced weapon, and an enhanced fighter on one short encounter. Compare a loadout-based deadline with an active-use signature meter followed by a response timer. | Select the model and readable consequences. Neither alternative is a timer requirement for the Roman opening. |
| Captors (P11) | An ambitious Tier Two civilization, constrained under a Great Armistice, is favored. | Settle identities and sponsorship; initial captors, buyer, employer, and ship command need not be identical. |
| Ship interlude (P12) | A simple menu-based hub for preparation, recovery, and relationships. | Define a minimal mission summary, loadout choice, and one interaction before facilities, favors, crafting, or multiple currencies. No hub is implemented here. |
| Progression (P13) | Equipment, skills/attributes, cybernetic augmentation; biological regeneration as an alternative. | Resolve signature effects, reversibility, injury costs, and campaign recoverability. Tactical Revive is not a long-term medical system. |
| Additional companions | Friendly alien and AI instructor concepts. | Roles, tone, relationships, and participation remain proposed; no translator-based loss of control is approved. |

## Suggested next experiment, not a committed feature

After evaluating the Roman mission, isolate a small power/detection encounter. Measure completion, objectives abandoned, turns, technology use, extraction outcomes, and whether players understand the tradeoff. Establish the system before authoring the alien veteran's lesson. No permanent augmentation, inevitable death, enforcement disintegration, or ship immunity should be inferred from the brainstorming notes.
