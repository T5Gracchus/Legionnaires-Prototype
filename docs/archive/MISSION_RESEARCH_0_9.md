# Objective research and implementation choices — build 0.9

> Archived source material. For current information use [the documentation index](../../README.md#documentation). Historical values and proposals below describe their original build.

Reviewed for the user's six-mission expansion. This is source-based research, not a claim of fresh hands-on play in the reference games. The local sibling decision log, mission brief, setting, and game-comparison worksheet were checked. The Roman trio, three separate action categories, biological traversal, and equipment/detection tradeoff remain intact. These mission names and values are prototype experiments, not new authoritative campaign lore.

The summit and linked sluices are our authored experiments, not reconstructions of a specific reference encounter. The summit tests keeping a holder alive during counterattacks; the sluices test splitting the squad for simultaneous control. Their gold map outlines show the eligible ground squares. Contest or an empty control pauses progress rather than wiping it out.

Destruction is deliberately bounded: weapon attacks break HP-based barricades and engines. A destroyed barricade becomes walkable debris and stops blocking sight/providing cover. Terrain height stays fixed; this build does not simulate collapsing floors, falling buildings, chain explosions, or physics. Compare breaching against walking around; remove any breach that becomes an obscure mandatory ammunition tax.

Battlefield props were created as original images using `image_gen.imagegen`. No reference-game artwork was copied into the game. Exact prompts and generated source locations are in `assets/battle/sources/mission-prompts.json`; packed props are project-local. Existing preparation/menu illustrations stay unchanged.

Human checks: can the player identify the goal before moving, read protected HP and incoming rounds, predict when charge advances, guide the cart without blocking its route, and finish the recommended loadout near ten active minutes? Automated completion rates and planning-speed estimates cannot answer those readability or human-duration questions.
